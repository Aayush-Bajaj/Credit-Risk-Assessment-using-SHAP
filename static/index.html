System Architecture

Institutional Underwriting Methodology

Engineered for robust credit risk estimation through cross-validated ensembles, parametric probability calibration, and empirical risk thresholding.

01

5-Fold Calibrated Ensemble

Applications are evaluated across five distinct gradient boosted decision tree models trained on stratified cross-validation folds, eliminating single-split bias and ensuring out-of-fold generalization.

  • Gradient boosted decision trees
  • Out-of-fold stratified training
  • In-memory parallel prediction
02

Platt Sigmoid Calibration

Raw ensemble margins are mapped into true posterior probabilities using calibrated sigmoid scaling: \(P = \frac{1}{1 + \exp(a \cdot z + b)}\). This guarantees reliable expected-loss estimations.

  • Platt isotonic scaling
  • Reliable posterior probabilities
  • Brier score optimization
03

Cost-Optimized Cutoff (75.6%)

The decision threshold is empirically determined at 75.6% default probability to minimize total lending loss, balancing the asymmetric cost of default against origination yield.

  • Asymmetric cost optimization
  • Transparent approval boundary
  • Auditable policy adherence
Governance & Operations

Underwriting Governance & FAQ

Standard operating procedures, explainability compliance, and algorithmic decision criteria.

What does the Calibrated Default Probability represent?

It represents the statistical probability (0.0% to 100.0%) that the applicant will fail to meet contractual loan obligations within the observation period. The probability is calibrated via Platt scaling so that a predicted score of 15% directly corresponds to an observed 15% historical default rate among similar cohorts.

How is the 75.6% decision threshold determined?

The 75.6% threshold is mathematically derived to optimize net portfolio returns given historical recovery rates, charge-offs, and underwriting costs. Applications with default probabilities below 75.6% are recommended for approval, while scores at or above the cutoff are flagged as high risk.

How are SHAP feature contributions used in Adverse Action Notices?

Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) and Equal Credit Opportunity Act (ECOA), lenders must provide specific key reasons when an application is denied. The localized SHAP decomposition identifies the exact applicant variables (e.g., debt-to-income ratio, prior defaults, loan grade) that contributed most heavily to the elevated risk score.

Is human underwriter discretion required?

Yes. This algorithmic assessment serves as a decision-support tool. Applications that sit within borderline risk bands (e.g., 50% to 75.6%) or involve non-standard financial profiles should undergo second-line review by an experienced credit underwriter.
